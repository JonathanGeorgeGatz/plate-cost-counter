The Plate Cost Counter — the three prompts, word for word

These are the exact prompts from Episode 27 of Build It With AI, in the order they were sent. Steal them. The tool they produced is here.

Prompt 1

I run a small restaurant and I want to know what each dish on my menu actually costs me to
make. I have three CSV exports: ingredients (ingredient, pack size, pack unit, pack price,
yield %), recipes (dish, menu price, ingredient, qty, unit), and sales (dish, sold). Build me
a single HTML file where I paste all three, type my target food cost percent, and click one
button. It should show what each plate costs, the food cost percentage against the menu
price, which dishes are over my target, and where last week's money went. One file, no
server, no uploads.

Prompt 2

Rebuild it, and this time follow these rules exactly. Units convert only inside their own
family, at exact factors: weight (g, kg, oz, lb — 1 lb = 453.592 g), volume (ml, l, tsp,
tbsp, fl oz, cup, qt, gal — 1 tbsp = 14.7868 ml, 1 fl oz = 29.5735 ml), count (each,
dozen = 12). A recipe line in a different family from its ingredient's pack unit is refused —
there is no density table, so do not guess. Yield divides: cost per usable base unit =
(pack price ÷ pack size in base units) ÷ (yield% ÷ 100). Names match after trimming
surrounding spaces and ignoring letter case. Menu price must agree to the cent across all of
a dish's rows; a conflict refuses the whole dish, and a missing or zero menu price refuses
the dish. A blank or non-numeric quantity refuses the line — blank is not zero. An exactly
duplicated row is parked visibly and counted once; two sales rows for one dish with
different counts are both refused and the dish shows "no trusted sales". Every refusal
names the pasted row number and gives the reason in plain words. A dish with a refused line
shows its partial cost and stays out of the totals and the money panel.

Prompt 3

Now make the page check itself before it asks to be believed. Validate each paste's header
row first and refuse a whole paste whose columns are wrong. Then count in public:
ingredient rows in = accepted + parked + refused; recipe lines in = costed + parked +
refused; sales rows in = accepted + parked + refused; and the money panel must re-add to
the cent, with revenue minus food cost equaling profit exactly. If any of those checks
fails, print DO NOT TRUST THESE PLATE COSTS across the top of the page and leave it up.
And fix what the independent checker caught in the last build: yield must DIVIDE the cost,
never multiply it; the tablespoon is 14.7868 ml, not 29.5735; and sales names must match
after trimming and case-folding, the same as everywhere else.
